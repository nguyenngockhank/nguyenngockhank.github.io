AI Knowledge

1. Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence

Definition and Core Purpose

  • Simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems.
  • Focuses on learning, reasoning, and self-correction.

Types of AI by Capability

Narrow AI or Weak AI

  • Designed for specific tasks like facial recognition or internet searches.
  • Examples include Apple Siri and Amazon Alexa.

General AI or Strong AI

  • Theoretical level of AI that can understand and learn any intellectual task a human can.
  • Currently remains a goal for future research and development.

Superintelligence

  • Hypothetical stage where AI surpasses human intelligence across all domains, including creativity and social skills.

2. Machine Learning Foundations

Supervised Learning

  • Definition: Training models on labeled datasets where the desired output is known.
  • Includes techniques like Linear Regression for predicting house prices.
  • Includes classification tasks like spam detection in emails.

Unsupervised Learning

  • Definition: Finding hidden patterns or structures in input data without explicit labels.
  • Clustering algorithms like K-Means used for customer segmentation in marketing.
  • Dimensionality reduction techniques like Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

Reinforcement Learning

  • Definition: Learning through trial and error using rewards and penalties.
  • An agent interacts with an environment to maximize cumulative reward.
  • Used in training AlphaGo to play the game of Go and in robotics for navigation.

3. Deep Learning and Neural Networks

Neural Network Architecture

Layers

  • Input layer: Receives the raw data.
  • Hidden layers: Perform mathematical transformations and feature extraction.
  • Output layer: Provides the final prediction or classification.

Specialized Architectures

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

  • Primarily used for processing grid-like data such as images.
  • Essential for facial recognition and medical imaging analysis.

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

  • Designed for sequential data where the order of information matters.
  • Used in time-series forecasting and speech recognition.

Transformers

  • Utilize attention mechanisms to weigh the importance of different parts of input data.
  • Basis for modern Large Language Models like GPT-4.

4. Natural Language Processing

Text Processing Tasks

  • Tokenization: Breaks text into individual words or sub-words.
  • Sentiment analysis: Determines the emotional tone of social media posts.
  • Named Entity Recognition (NER): Identifies people, places, and organizations in text.

Language Translation

  • Neural Machine Translation systems like Google Translate.
  • Real-time cross-lingual communication tools.

Conversational AI

  • Chatbots used in customer service for automated support.
  • Virtual assistants capable of understanding complex voice commands.

5. Generative AI

Large Language Models (LLMs)

  • Models trained on massive datasets to generate human-like text.
  • Applications include code generation with GitHub Copilot and creative writing.

Image and Media Generation

  • Image generation: Diffusion models used to create realistic images from text prompts.
    • Examples include Midjourney, DALL-E 3, and Stable Diffusion.
  • Video generation: Tools like OpenAI Sora for creating cinematic scenes.

6. Computer Vision

Image Classification

  • Categorizes an entire image into a specific class, such as identifying a breed of dog.

Object Detection

  • Identifies and locates multiple objects within a single frame.
  • Algorithms like YOLO used in autonomous vehicle obstacle detection.

Image Segmentation

  • Partitions an image into multiple segments at the pixel level.
  • Used in self-driving cars to distinguish between the road, pedestrians, and sidewalks.

7. AI Ethics and Governance

Bias and Fairness

  • Addressing data bias that leads to discriminatory outcomes in hiring or law enforcement.
  • Implementing fairness audits for algorithmic transparency.

Privacy and Security

  • Protecting user data used for training AI models.
  • Risks of deepfakes used for misinformation or identity theft.

Safety and Alignment

  • Ensuring AI systems act in accordance with human values and intentions.
  • Preventing hallucinations where AI provides confident but false information.

8. Practical Industry Applications

Healthcare

  • AI-driven drug discovery for identifying new molecular structures.
  • Early detection of cancers through AI-enhanced X-ray and MRI analysis.

Finance

  • Fraud detection systems that monitor credit card transactions in real time.
  • Algorithmic trading platforms that execute high-frequency stock trades.

Transportation

  • Autonomous driving systems developed by companies like Tesla and Waymo.
  • Route optimization for logistics and delivery services like UPS and FedEx.
